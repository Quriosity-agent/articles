## Four-Stage Full Pipeline Breakdown

### Stage 1: Development

**From blank page to green light.**

- Generate character visual anchors with Nano Banana 2 (detailed prompts → consistent character imagery)
- Use Kling for cross-scene character consistency (up to 5 characters + 14 objects)
- Go from a logline to a 20-frame lookbook in an afternoon
- **Pitch meetings shift from "imagine my film" to "here IS my film"**

What AI can't do: Tell you if your story matters. Emotional specificity still comes from humans.

### Stage 2: Pre-production

**From approved script to ready-to-shoot.**

- **invideo Vision Boards**: natural language → 3×3 storyboard grids, 9 shots generated at once
- **Previsualization**: Blender rough blocking → Nano Banana texturing → Kling animation
- What took a previz team weeks can now take one filmmaker hours

Critical instruction: Specify that "facial structure, proportions, and identity must remain exactly consistent across all panels."

### Stage 3: Production

**Capturing (or generating) footage.**

- **Camera Controls**: dolly, pan, tilt, track, jib, drone sweep, crash zoom — calculated with real depth and parallax from a single frame
- **World building without sets**: Nano Banana generates environments → Kling Motion Control adds movement
- **invideo Performances**: Feed in real actor performance → preserves micro-expressions, breath, hesitation → transform character and world
- Most reliable method: lock master plate (still frame) first, then animate

What AI can't do: The full spectrum of human performance. Micro-expressions, the hesitation before a line — still best captured by a real human.

### Stage 4: Post-production

**Raw material → something with rhythm, emotion, and meaning.**

- **VFX House**: background swaps, prop replacement, character swaps, motion control — within the generative pipeline
- **UltraRes**: Unify different resolution footage (AI-generated / archival / live-action) to consistent quality
- Honest truth: professional delivery still finishes in Premiere/After Effects/DaVinci Resolve

## Core Builder Insights

### 1) "Floor risen, ceiling unmoved"

The most important framing:
- **Floor**: Technical execution costs collapse (compositing, rotoscoping, color correction, motion graphics)
- **Ceiling**: Taste, judgment, emotional intelligence — these determine the upper limit

### 2) Tool chains have evolved from "point tools" to "pipelines"

Not "use one AI tool" but "orchestrate an AI pipeline":
- Text → Image → Video → Post → Export
- Pick the best tool at each node, chain them together

### 3) The director's core skill has changed

> "The director's most important skill is no longer just knowing where to put the camera. It's knowing how to orchestrate intelligent tools that move as fast as their imagination."
