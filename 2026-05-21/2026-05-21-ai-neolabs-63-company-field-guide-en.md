The map groups 63 highly valued AI “neolabs” that are mostly pre-product-market-fit and sub-revenue-scale, but are capitalized like future platform companies. Their shared pattern is not simply “building another LLM”; each is trying to move AI research into a more specific control point: robotics, world models, scientific discovery, mathematics, inference infrastructure, enterprise agents, voice, video, or brain-computer interfaces.

Below is a one-paragraph description of what each company appears to be doing, in the same order as the chart. Many are still stealth or only partially public, so these summaries are grounded in the chart’s founders / known-for / category fields plus public positioning where available.

## One paragraph per company

### 01. Roze (Robotics)

A SoftBank-led robotics-AI initiative from Masayoshi Son, aiming less at another chatbot and more at recombining large models, embodied intelligence, and SoftBank’s robotics assets for the physical world.

### 02. Thinking Machines Lab (Frontier lab)

A frontier-model lab founded by ex-OpenAI and PyTorch figures including Mira Murati, John Schulman, and Soumith Chintala, focused on new human-model interaction patterns as much as raw model scale.

### 03. Project Prometheus (Robotics)

A Jeff Bezos-backed AI operating company pointed at the physical economy: moving AI from software assistance into manufacturing, warehousing, logistics, and robotic execution.

### 04. SSI (Safe Superintelligence) (Frontier lab)

Ilya Sutskever’s safe-superintelligence lab, whose “product” is not a near-term API but a long-horizon research program for safely training frontier AGI/ASI systems.

### 05. Reflection AI (Frontier lab / Open source)

Founded by ex-DeepMind researchers, Reflection AI is trying to build U.S.-based open frontier models, balancing open weights, reasoning capability, and enterprise controllability.

### 06. Skild AI (Robotics)

A robotics-foundation-model company from CMU professors Deepak Pathak and Abhinav Gupta, aiming to let many robot bodies share perception, control, and manipulation skills instead of hand-coding each machine.

### 07. Poolside (Frontier lab / Coding)

A coding-model company from the GitHub Copilot lineage, training software-engineering-specific models and developer environments rather than simply plugging generic chat models into IDEs.

### 08. Physical Intelligence (Robotics)

A general robot-intelligence company from Stanford/Google robotics researchers, training transferable physical skills from web-scale data, simulation, and real-world manipulation.

### 09. Periodic Labs (Bio / Materials)

A materials-science AI lab involving Liam Fedus and Ekin Dogus Cubuk, using models to accelerate materials discovery by turning experimentation into a plan-generate-verify loop.

### 10. Ineffable Intelligence (Frontier lab)

David Silver’s new lab, likely centered on reinforcement learning, search, and general reasoning—an extension of the DeepMind playbook of solving hard problems before packaging products.

### 11. World Labs (World models)

Fei-Fei Li’s spatial-intelligence company building world models so AI can understand 3D scenes, object relations, and actionable spatial structure rather than merely labeling images.

### 12. Recursive Superintelligence (Frontier lab / Continual)

Founded by Richard Socher, Tim Rocktäschel, Josh Tobin, and Jeff Clune, this lab is about self-improving AI: continual learning, tool use, and automated research loops that upgrade the system itself.

### 13. AMI Labs (World models)

A European world-model company associated with Yann LeCun and Alexandre LeBrun, likely extending LeCun’s JEPA/predictive-learning view of non-autoregressive world modeling.

### 14. Unconventional AI (Compute)

Naveen Rao’s post-MosaicML company, focused less on an application and more on redesigning the compute, chip, or systems stack behind AI training and inference.

### 15. Humans& (Frontier lab)

A research-first lab from Eric Zelikman and Georges Harik, combining xAI-style research with early Google organizational experience to explore stronger reasoning, synthetic data, and autonomous research systems.

### 16. Core Automation (Frontier lab / Continual)

Former OpenAI VP Jerry Tworek’s “most automated AI lab,” focused on automating research, experiments, evaluation, and training workflows so the lab itself becomes a high-throughput machine.

### 17. Inflection AI (Enterprise AI)

An enterprise-AI company founded by Mustafa Suleyman, Karen Simonyan, and Reid Hoffman; after Pi, much of the team and technology shifted toward Microsoft, leaving an enterprise-assistant/model-service trajectory.

### 18. Hark (Enterprise AI / Personal AI hardware)

A Brett Adcock-funded AI effort. The original map places it near enterprise AI, but the Crossing article adds a more consumer-facing read: Hark appears aimed at personal-AI hardware and a general interaction interface, making it one of the rarer Neo Labs pointed directly at a consumer entry point.

### 19. Ricursive Intelligence (Hardware / Chip design)

Founded by Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini, Ricursive Intelligence applies AI to chip design by automating layout, architecture search, and optimization. It should not be confused with Richard Socher’s Recursive Superintelligence: Ricursive is about an AI-designed-chip feedback loop, while Recursive Superintelligence is about recursively self-improving AI.

### 20. Xaira Therapeutics (Bio / Drug discovery)

A drug-discovery company involving Marc Tessier-Lavigne, David Baker, and others, combining generative models, protein design, and wet-lab infrastructure into an AI-native biotech pipeline.

### 21. Isomorphic Labs (Bio / Drug discovery)

DeepMind/Alphabet’s drug-discovery spinout, built on AlphaFold-era capabilities to connect structural biology, molecular design, and pharmaceutical partnerships.

### 22. Decart (World models)

The world-model company behind Mirage and Oasis, turning video and interactive environments into realtime explorable worlds—moving from watching generated video to inhabiting generated spaces.

### 23. Sakana AI (Frontier lab / Efficient)

A Japan-based lab from David Ha, Llion Jones, and Ren Ito, emphasizing efficient, evolutionary, composable AI systems and AI-for-science workflows.

### 24. General Intuition (World models)

A world-model company training on massive gameplay-video datasets so AI can learn objects, actions, feedback, and strategy before transferring to simulation and real-world tasks.

### 25. Liquid AI (Frontier lab / Efficient)

An MIT spinout around liquid neural networks and efficient on-device models, targeting low-latency, low-power AI for edge and enterprise settings where huge cloud models are overkill.

### 26. H (The H Company) (Enterprise AI)

A French model company from ex-DeepMind researchers, targeting enterprise agents and automated workflows packaged for deployable company processes.

### 27. Rhoda AI (Robotics)

The robotics company behind the FutureVision robot platform, integrating robot hardware, perception, and learned control into a scalable robotics stack.

### 28. Axiom Math (Math)

Carina Hong’s formal-math company, focused on Putnam-style problem solving and verifiable mathematical reasoning through proof, search, and generalization.

### 29. Flapping Airplanes (Frontier lab)

A young research-first team from Ben Spector, Asher Spector, and Aidan Smith, built around contrarian research bets that challenge the default scale-only assumptions of major labs.

### 30. Magic (Frontier lab / Coding)

A European long-context coding-model company aiming to let models understand large codebases, maintain project state, and complete cross-file software-engineering tasks.

### 31. Harmonic (Math)

A math-superintelligence company backed by Robinhood founder Vlad Tenev, using formal verification and automated proof to turn mathematical reasoning into reliable machine capability.

### 32. AI21 Labs (Enterprise AI)

An Israeli LLM company behind Jurassic/Jamba and enterprise writing tools, positioning around business-grade language models, long context, and controllable generation.

### 33. Lila Sciences (Bio / Lab automation)

Geoffrey von Maltzahn’s AI-science/lab-automation company, connecting experiment design, robotic execution, data feedback, and model updates into a closed loop.

### 34. Chai Discovery (Bio / Drug discovery)

An OpenAI-backed biotech unicorn applying generative AI to drug discovery, molecular design, protein design, and candidate validation workflows.

### 35. Goodfire (Interpretability)

Founded by interpretability researchers from top labs, Goodfire builds tools for understanding model internals and turning that understanding into safety and product-control mechanisms.

### 36. EvolutionaryScale (Bio / Drug discovery)

An ex-Meta research team behind the ESM3 protein language model, aiming to generate, edit, and search protein sequences and structures as fluidly as language.

### 37. Moonvalley (Video)

A video-generation company training the licensed-data model Marey, differentiating through copyright-safe data and professional creative workflows rather than only viral social clips.

### 38. Aaru (Simulation)

A simulation company with a headline $1B Series A, building simulated worlds for training, evaluation, and scenario analysis so AI can learn cheaply before acting in reality.

### 39. Simile (Simulation)

From the Stanford agent-simulation lineage of Joon Park, Michael Bernstein, and Percy Liang, Simile focuses on social simulation, multi-agent behavior, and observable human/organizational systems.

### 40. Ndea (Frontier lab / Program synthesis)

François Chollet and Zapier founder Mike Knoop’s program-synthesis lab, focused on AI that constructs programs and abstractions rather than merely completing text.

### 41. Imbue (Enterprise AI)

An early AI-lab unicorn focused on agents, reasoning, and developer-facing AI tools, trying to make models reliably complete multi-step tasks.

### 42. AAI (Frontier lab)

A stealth lab from Mobileye figures Amnon Shashua and Shai Shalev-Shwartz, likely translating experience in vision, autonomous driving, and learning theory into frontier AI systems.

### 43. Reka (Frontier lab / Multimodal)

A multimodal frontier-model company from ex-Google researchers, building text, image, audio/video understanding and generation capabilities for enterprise deployment.

### 44. Kyutai (Voice)

A European open-source AI lab focused on realtime voice models, low-latency interaction, and multilingual assistants as an EU-centered alternative path.

### 45. Prime Intellect (Decentralized AI training)

A decentralized training, inference, and RL infrastructure company that coordinates distributed compute to train open models, challenging the centralized GPU-cluster model.

### 46. Arcee AI (Frontier lab / Open source)

An enterprise small-language-model company building SLMs, merge tooling, and Arcee Cloud so companies can run cheaper, more controllable, more private model stacks.

### 47. Essential AI (Frontier lab / Open source)

Founded by Transformer authors Ashish Vaswani and Niki Parmar, Essential AI builds AI workers for enterprise tasks across documents, spreadsheets, and business operations.

### 48. Zyphra (Frontier lab / Open source)

Known for strong small-model results and the Maia stack, Zyphra trains efficient, open, deployable models that make smaller parameter counts feel practical in real tasks.

### 49. Mirendil (Frontier lab / AI research)

Founded by ex-Anthropic scientific-reasoning researchers Behnam Neyshabur and Harsh Mehta, Mirendil targets scientific reasoning and research automation: discovery, proof, and experiment planning.

### 50. Nous Research (Agents)

A leading decentralized-training and open-model research group, building open-weight models, RL systems, agents, and community-driven research collaboration.

### 51. Merge Labs (Brain-computer interface)

A Sam Altman-backed non-invasive BCI spinout, aiming for higher-bandwidth, lower-friction interfaces between brain signals and AI systems.

### 52. Inferact (Inference)

Founded by vLLM/open-source inference veterans including Simon Mo, Woosuk Kwon, and Kaichao You, Inferact commercializes high-performance LLM serving and throughput optimization.

### 53. Isara (Frontier lab / Future prediction)

An OpenAI-backed future-prediction company with 2,000-agent demos, using large-scale agent simulations to model markets, organizations, and event evolution.

### 54. Inception Labs (Frontier lab / Diffusion coding)

A diffusion-LLM company associated with Stanford’s Stefano Ermon, exploring diffusion generation as an alternative to autoregressive decoding, especially for coding and complex text tasks.

### 55. Elorian (Frontier lab / Multimodal)

Founded by ex-DeepMind executives, Elorian targets visual reasoning: helping multimodal models understand complex images, spatial relations, and reasoning-heavy visual tasks.

### 56. Standard Intelligence (Frontier lab / Computer Use)

A computer-use model company from Galen Mead and Devansh Pandey; FDM-1 is video-first, training AI to operate computers by learning from screen recordings.

### 57. Adaption Labs (Frontier lab / Continual)

Founded by ex-Cohere executives, Adaption Labs focuses on continual learning and enterprise model adaptation so models can keep updating with company data, workflows, and feedback.

### 58. Poetiq (Agents)

An ex-DeepMind team building agents with strong ARC-AGI-style results, focused on abstract reasoning, tool use, and reliable long-horizon task performance.

### 59. Genesis AI (Robotics)

A French ex-Mistral/Skild robotics team building embodied AI and robot foundation models that connect manipulation, simulation, learned control, and real robot training.

### 60. CuspAI (Bio / Materials)

An AI-chemistry company from Chad Edwards and Max Welling, using generative models and materials search to design new molecules, adsorbents, and industrial chemistry materials.

### 61. RadixArk (Inference)

Founded by Ying Sheng and Banghua Zhu from the SGLang/LMSYS/xAI lineage, RadixArk commercializes efficient inference systems to lower LLM serving costs.

### 62. QuTwo (Quantum)

Founded by former AMD Silo AI CEO Peter Sarlin, QuTwo carries a Finland/EU sovereign-AI flavor and points toward the quantum-AI intersection or next-generation compute platforms.

### 63. Sooth Labs (Frontier lab / Future prediction)

A probabilistic-forecasting lab from Chuck Hoover, Yaser Sheikh, and Ruslan Salakhutdinov, training models to represent distributions over future events rather than just produce answers.

## What the map really says

First, capital is splitting the “AI lab” template into many vertical research companies: robotics, materials, drug discovery, math, video, voice, chip design, simulation, and inference are all absorbing talent from the major labs. Second, these valuations are not justified by current revenue; they are bets on who will control a new platform layer if a foundational capability breaks through. Third, the key question is not which company has the best demo today, but which control surface it owns: data, compute, hardware, architecture, evaluation, lab automation, or distribution.

In one sentence: these are not 63 chatbot startups; they are 63 bets on where the next AI platform layer will land—physical reality, scientific experimentation, software engineering, inference infrastructure, or human-computer interfaces.

## Crossing update: another read on the 20 most valuable Neo Labs

The Crossing article does not cover the full 63-company map. Instead, it cuts the category by the 20 most valuable Silicon Valley-style Neo Labs and adds a useful thesis for why this company type is emerging in 2025-2026: research outputs can now become code, molecules, materials designs, or operating plans directly; large-model tooling lets researchers prototype without waiting for large engineering teams; and capital is increasingly willing to fund research directions before products or revenue appear.

Three additions are especially worth folding into this field guide:

1. **Project Prometheus is the clearest archetype.** It has no public product yet, but reportedly started with roughly $6.2B in capital and was later reported to be nearing a roughly $10B round at around a $38B post-money valuation. The center of gravity is not chat; it is engineering, manufacturing, and the physical economy.
2. **Hark may be more consumer-facing than the map suggests.** The Crossing article frames it around personal-AI hardware and a general interaction interface, not just enterprise automation.
3. **Ricursive Intelligence and Recursive Superintelligence are different companies.** Ricursive is the Anna Goldie / Azalia Mirhoseini AI-chip-design company; Recursive Superintelligence is Richard Socher’s self-improving-AI company.

The article’s risk framing is also important: the real test is whether these companies survive the silent period. Drug pipelines, chip tape-outs, robotics foundation models, and world models take years to validate; meanwhile, Meta, Google, OpenAI, and other incumbents can pull researchers back with extraordinary compensation and accelerated vesting. Neo Lab valuations are betting on decade-scale breakthroughs, but talent and capital markets reprice the category every few months.
