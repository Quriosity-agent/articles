# Writing Polish

Use this reference for the final editing pass on articles.

## Goals

- Preserve meaning, facts, source dates, and caveats.
- Make the article sound like a human analytical note, not a generated summary.
- Keep the piece useful for builders: what changed, why it matters, what workflow it affects, and what to verify.

## Editing Order

1. Fix structure first:
   - Make the thesis clear early.
   - Group related claims under purposeful headings.
   - Remove repeated points and generic setup.
2. Strengthen evidence:
   - Name concrete products, models, files, benchmarks, workflows, or UI states.
   - Separate source claims from interpretation.
   - Add caveats where evidence is partial, dynamic, gated, or unverified.
3. Polish sentences:
   - Replace vague transitions with specific causal links.
   - Cut filler such as "not just X, but Y" when repeated too often.
   - Avoid hype unless the source evidence supports it.
   - Prefer direct verbs and concrete nouns.
4. Match bilingual intent:
   - The English version should read naturally, not as a literal translation.
   - Preserve technical terms consistently across both versions.

## Anti-Slop Checklist

- No invented citations, claims, dates, or rankings.
- No empty "this changes everything" framing.
- No generic conclusion that could fit any AI product.
- No unexplained benchmark numbers.
- No image references without local files.
- No source-media omission when the original page is visual and the user asked to include images.
